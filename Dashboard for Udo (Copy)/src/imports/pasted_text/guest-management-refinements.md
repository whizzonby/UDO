The top section of the page is close, but it is missing a sense of immediate context. Right now, the title and “Add Guest” button exist in isolation, which makes the page feel slightly empty and forces the user to scan downward to understand where they are. This should be adjusted by introducing a single line of contextual information directly beneath the title that anchors both scale and urgency. This line should read along the lines of “120 guests · RSVP deadline June 1 (12 days left)” and should be styled as soft, secondary text that does not compete visually with the title. The intention here is not to add noise, but to remove uncertainty. The user should immediately understand how many guests they are managing and how close they are to a key milestone without having to think about it.

The summary cards are one of the strongest elements on the page, but they are currently underutilized and slightly unclear in definition. The biggest issue is the “Needs Attention” card, which overlaps conceptually with “Awaiting RSVP” and introduces confusion. This should be replaced entirely with “Incomplete Guests,” which clearly communicates that these are guests who have responded but still require additional information. The subtext must be precise and should read “Missing meal, travel, or key details.” Each card must have a distinct responsibility: Total Guests reflects scale, Confirmed reflects attendance, Awaiting RSVP reflects non-response, and Incomplete Guests reflects missing data. In addition, all cards must be made clickable and act as entry points into filtered guest lists. This transforms them from passive statistics into actionable navigation, which is essential for making the page feel purposeful.

The “Guest Readiness” section is conceptually correct but currently feels slightly mechanical and contributes to the sense that the page is analytical rather than calming. This should first be renamed to “Guest Progress,” which softens the tone and aligns better with the emotional positioning of the product. The labels within this section should be simplified so they read naturally, for example “RSVPs received,” “Meals selected,” “Travel details added,” and “Seating assigned.” The supporting text beneath the bars should shift away from neutral reporting and instead provide reassurance, such as “You are making great progress. Most guests have responded, and key details are coming together.” Visually, the bars should be softened by reducing contrast, increasing spacing, and using rounded edges so that the section feels gentle and supportive rather than performance-driven. The goal is for the user to feel that things are under control, not being evaluated.

The “What matters most right now” section is directionally strong but needs to evolve into a more intelligent and emotionally aware guidance layer. This section should be renamed to “What to focus on next” to better reflect its purpose. The language should be rewritten so that it feels like a calm nudge rather than an instruction. For example, instead of sounding directive, it should say something like “You are doing well. A few guests still need to respond, and some meal details are missing. This is a good time to follow up and complete those details.” The tone must always feel reassuring and supportive, never urgent or corrective. This section should also include one or two clear actions, such as “View pending guests” or “Review guest details,” which connect insight directly to action without overwhelming the user.

A key gap in the current page is the absence of a clear execution layer, which makes the experience feel passive. To resolve this, a “Take action” section should be introduced directly below the guidance section. This section should contain a small set of high-value actions such as sending RSVP reminders, viewing pending responses, completing guest details, and assigning seating. These should be presented as clean, evenly spaced buttons that are easy to tap and visually consistent with the rest of the interface. This addition is critical because it bridges the gap between awareness and action, allowing the user to immediately respond to what the page is telling them without needing to navigate elsewhere.

The page is also missing any form of lightweight guest segmentation, which becomes increasingly important as the number of guests grows. A small “Guest Groups” section should be introduced, displaying simple counts such as “Wedding Party” and “Travelling Guests.” This should be visually minimal, using small cards or pill-style elements that do not compete with more important sections. The purpose of this addition is not to add complexity, but to introduce structure and make the system feel more aligned with how users naturally think about their guest list.

The “Recent Activity” section should be retained but refined significantly. In its current form, it risks becoming noise because it includes low-value updates such as guests opening links. This should be replaced with only meaningful, decision-relevant insights, such as the number of guests still pending RSVP, the number missing meal choices, or newly added guests. The goal is for this section to function as a subtle awareness layer that reinforces what still needs attention, rather than a log of minor system events.

Finally, the “Preview guest views” element should be removed entirely from this page. It does not belong in the overview context and distracts from the primary purpose of the screen, which is to provide clarity and direction. This feature should instead live within the Invitations or Guest Experience section, where it is more relevant and actionable.

Overall, these changes do not add complexity but instead reduce friction by sharpening definitions, improving hierarchy, and introducing clear pathways from insight to action. The result should be a page that feels calm, intelligent, and immediately useful, where the user understands their position, feels reassured about their progress, and knows exactly what to do next without having to think.
